# Learner exercise authoring standard

Apply this standard to every new learner-facing exercise and assessment in this project: vocabulary, reading, listening, matching, gap-fill, dialogue or simulation, pronunciation, grammar, and final checks.

- Use believable everyday, study, or work contexts: messages, bookings, introductions, short dialogues, notices, practical choices, or useful profile updates.
- Do not ask a learner to name, identify, or explain a grammar rule in a prompt.
- Put grammar into the natural language of the scenario. If the learner answers correctly, give a short contextual explanation that may name the relevant rule.
- Avoid abstract true/false statements about language rules. Replace them with one unambiguous contextual statement or another practical response format.
- Give every question one defensible answer. Make the context precise enough to rule out alternatives, and remove options that remain plausible.
- Keep learner-facing content free of personal data, source transcripts, and meeting identifiers.
- Make each homework a separately selectable archive item with its own overview, lesson notes, and section tabs.
- For a standard homework package, prepare 10 grammar questions, 10 vocabulary questions, one short reading with 3 true/false and 2 multiple-choice checks, 3 listening exercises or questions from a licensed external learning source, and a 10-question final multiple-choice quiz.
- Keep all student instructions in English, while French examples and prompts remain in meaningful French.
- Prefer crisp CSS/vector styling over low-resolution raster background images.
